# 4f electrons in a crystal field

Suppose we have a crystal field generated by a group of ligand atoms:
$$
V_{\text{CF}}(\mathbf{r}) =\sum\limits _{\mathbf{R}}\frac{-Z_{\mathbf{R}}^{*} e^{2}}{| \mathbf{r} -\mathbf{R}| }
$$
Set the position zero as the rare earth atom, then the ligands sit outside the range of the 4f electrons, that is, $r< R$, we can then do a multipole expansion:
$$
V(\mathbf{r}) =\sum\limits _{\mathbf{R}}\sum\limits _{lm}\frac{r^{l}}{R^{l+1}}\frac{4\pi }{2l+1} Y_{l}^{m*}(\hat{\mathbf{R}}) Y_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r})
$$
and then we condense the ligand dependence into a single coeffeicient $A_{lm}$, which equals
$$
\tilde{A}_{lm} =\sum\limits _{\mathbf{R}}\frac{1}{R^{l+1}}\frac{4\pi }{2l+1} Y_{l}^{m*}(\hat{\mathbf{R}})
$$
so that:
$$
V_{\text{CF}}(\mathbf{r}) =\sum\limits _{lm}\tilde{A}_{lm} r^{l} Y_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r})
$$
This is the potential for a single electron, so the many-body Hamiltonian writes
$$
\hat{H} =\sum\limits _{i}\sum\limits _{lm} A_{lm} r^{l} Y_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r}_{i})
$$
where $i$ labels the electrons.

In the literature, the unormalized spherical harmonics $C_{l}^{m}$ are often used, which equals
$$
C_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r}) =\left(\frac{4\pi }{2l+1}\right)^{1/2} Y_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r})
$$
so instead we write
$$
V_{\text{CF}}(\mathbf{r}) =\sum\limits _{lm} A_{lm} r^{l} C_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r})
$$


In rare-earth ions, strong spin-orbit coupling of 4f electrons makes $|L,S,J\rangle $ good quantum numbers to describe the ground state, which obeys the Hund's rules. Since the $( 2J+1)$-dimensional spin subspace is the space of ground state, and will split under the crystal field, we wish to project $\hat{H}$ onto this $( 2J+1)$-subspace and get its form. The detailed parameters can then be fitted from the experiments or calculations.

# Stevens Operator

Let us look at what symmetries restrict the form of Stevens operators, since we are considering 4f electrons, we need to consider matrix elements like:
$$
\langle r^{l} \rangle \langle 3m|C_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r}) |3m^{\prime } \rangle 
$$
Using the Wigner-Eckart theorem and parity, we know that
$$
l=2n,\ 0\leqslant l\leqslant 6
$$
so the only possible $l$'s are
$$
l=2,4,6
$$
(can also be proved by TRS)

Let's look closely at the Wigner Eckart theorem:
$$
\langle JM|C_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r}) |JM^{\prime } \rangle =\frac{\langle J||C_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r}) ||J\rangle }{\sqrt{2J+1}} \times \langle Jl;Mm|Jl;JM\rangle 
$$
However, a tensor operator $T_{l}^{m}$ constructed by the roral angular momentum operator yields the same relation, thus we can substitude this operator $C_{l}^{m}(\mathbf{r})$ with $T_{l}^{m}$ times some constant $\theta _{l}$, thus:
$$
H_{\text{CF}} =\sum\limits _{l=2,4,6}\sum\limits _{m} B_{l}^{m}\mathcal{O}_{l}^{m}
$$
where the form of $\mathcal{O}_{l}^{m}$ could be found in a table. For example, the $\mathcal{O}_{2}^{0}$ corresponds to the spherical harmonics $Y_{20} r^{2} =3z^{2} -r^{2}$, we know that $\mathbf{J}$ is a vector operatos, so we can do
$$
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{0} =3J_{z}^{2} -J( J+1)
$$

# Role of Symmetry

If the local environment of the rare-earth ion holds a specific point group symmetry, only a point-group-invariant $Y_{lm}$ or $B_{l}^{m}$ is allowed. Suppose a $C_{6}$ is present, then we can only have $m=0,\pm 6$, so the hexagonal Stevens operator is:
$$
H_{\text{CF}} =B_{2}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{2}^{0} +B_{4}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{4}^{0} +B_{6}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{6}^{0} +B_{6}^{6}\mathcal{O}_{6}^{6} +B_{6}^{-6}\mathcal{O}_{6}^{-6}
$$
If there is a $v$-mirror, $Y_{6}^{-6} -Y_{6}^{6}$ is odd under $\phi \rightarrow -\phi $, thus only the even term of $m=\pm 6$ remains. We often write:
$$
H_{\text{CF}} =B_{2}^{0} O_{2}^{0} +B_{4}^{0} O_{4}^{0} +B_{6}^{0} O_{6}^{0} +B_{6}^{6} O_{6}^{6}
$$
in this case, and this $O_{6}^{6}$ is a real operator $\frac{1}{2}\left( J_{+}^{6} +J_{-}^{6}\right)$.
